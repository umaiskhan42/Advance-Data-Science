# Anomaly detection as a WWTP component — with vs without

Generated by `scripts/wwtp_impact_sim.py`. This answers: *if the microfauna
anomaly detector is embedded in the plant's monitoring/control loop, what changes
versus operating without it?*

## What is modelled

An illustrative, literature-grounded **scenario** simulation (not a calibrated
plant model) of one sludge-degradation episode over 90 days. The premise is the
Sludge Biotic Index logic (Madoni, 1994): the microfauna community shifts toward
stressed taxa (nematodes, testate amoebae) **before** effluent quality fails, so
microfauna are a *leading* indicator while effluent TSS is a *lagging* one.

Two operating policies share the same disturbance:

- **Without AD (reactive):** the operator acts only when the **effluent** itself
  violates the discharge limit (lagging).
- **With AD (proactive):** our DINOv3/PatchCore detector images the sludge daily
  and raises an alarm when the flagged-stressed fraction rises. Crucially, the
  detector's reliability is its **real operating point** read from
  `results/csv/threshold_metrics.csv` — TPR = 0.678, FPR = 0.244 (Youden-J).

Process couplings (health → SVI → effluent TSS, and aeration energy scaling with
poor sludge state) are documented in the script header and constants.

## Result

| KPI | Without AD | With AD | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action day (early warning) | 34 | 24 | **10 days earlier** |
| Effluent-limit violation days | 4 | 0 | **−4 (full compliance)** |
| Cumulative excess TSS (mg/L·d) | 7.5 | 0.0 | −7.5 |
| Days in bulking (SVI > 150) | 12 | 0 | **bulking avoided** |
| Peak SVI (mL/g) | 186.7 | 137.5 | −49.2 |
| Peak effluent TSS (mg/L) | 32.6 | 18.0 | −14.6 |
| Aeration energy (kWh) | 99,196 | 96,186 | **−3,010 (~3%)** |
| Energy cost ($) | 11,904 | 11,542 | −362 |
| Compliance penalty ($) | 2,000 | 0 | −2,000 |
| **Total operating cost ($/episode)** | **13,904** | **11,542** | **−2,362** |

## Interpretation (effect on the treatment process)

1. **Early warning (10 days).** Because microfauna stress precedes effluent
   failure, the detector flags the episode while it is still correctable, before
   the discharge limit is breached.
2. **Effluent compliance.** Proactive correction keeps effluent TSS under the
   30 mg/L limit (0 violation-days vs 4), avoiding regulatory penalties.
3. **Bulking avoided.** Peak SVI stays below the 150 mL/g bulking line, so the
   plant never enters the costly filamentous-bulking regime (0 vs 12 days).
4. **Energy & cost.** A shallower, shorter episode needs less compensatory
   aeration (~3% energy), and total operating cost drops ~17% for the episode
   once avoided penalties are included.

## How this ties back to the lab and the proposal

This is the concrete realisation of hypothesis **H4** and of the project's
framing as an **extension of EMSEL's anomaly-detection work**: the same
"model-the-normal, flag-the-deviation" component the lab builds for WWTP/IAQ
*sensors* is here a *vision* front-end whose output drives the same kind of
monitoring/aeration-management loop the lab studies (e.g., RL aeration control in
sequencing batch reactors, Kim et al. 2026). The detector is not the end product —
it is one sensor in the plant's control loop, and this simulation quantifies the
value it adds there.

> Caveat: parameters are illustrative and chosen to reflect published SBI/bulking
> relationships; absolute kWh/$ are not plant-calibrated. The *relative* effect —
> earlier action, compliance, avoided bulking — follows directly from microfauna
> being a leading indicator plus the detector's measured TPR/FPR.
